# Rey’s / Azul — photography and repair-story brief

## Photography session

Shoot horizontal originals at least 3000 pixels wide. Supply the originals without text overlays, filters, or compression from messaging apps. Keep the same natural color treatment across the set.

1. **Hero:** a technician at the lift, framed wide with room on the left for a headline. Capture a 10-second steady take with an easy loop and a separate portrait crop for mobile.
2. **Team:** the actual team at the shop entrance; one relaxed group portrait and one candid shot. Capture faces clearly and keep distracting signage out of the frame.
3. **Diagnostics:** scan tool in use, technician and vehicle both visible. Avoid customer records on the screen.
4. **Maintenance:** oil service, filter replacement, and hands working on a real vehicle. Include a close-up and a wider context shot.
5. **German vehicles:** a clean three-quarter vehicle view and one repair detail. This can replace the current illustrative Porsche photo.
6. **Location:** entrance as a first-time customer sees it from the street, plus the service desk and waiting area if available.
7. **Repair stories:** matching before/after angles of the actual part or repair, only when the difference can be clearly explained.

Record six to ten seconds per video shot. Use a tripod or deliberate, slow handheld movement. Shoot during a normal service session with the team’s permission.

## Repair-story intake

Collect these facts for each client-approved story before it appears on the site:

- Vehicle year, make, and model.
- What the customer reported, in their own words if permission is given.
- What the inspection actually found.
- Work that was approved and performed.
- How the result was checked.
- Two to four original photos and a plain-language caption for each.
- Any customer quote the client has approved for publication.

Use those facts to write a short page: **Concern → Findings → Repair → Result**. Avoid publishing guessed causes, stock images as evidence of completed work, or invented time/cost savings.

## Reviews

Ask the client for the direct Google Business Profile link and three approved review excerpts, with author display names and original review links. The site currently links visitors to Google rather than displaying invented testimonials.
